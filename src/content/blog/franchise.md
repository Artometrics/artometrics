<div class="art-callout" id="findings">
<p class="art-p"><strong>Read this before the big numbers get comfortable.</strong> The figures below are nominal estimates of what audiences spent, compiled by Wikipedia's editors from press and trade sources over many years. They are not profit, not a company valuation, and not cash paid to the rights holder. A very large number can still be answering a different question than the one you meant to ask. Data vintage: Wikipedia revision 1384334611, timestamp 7 November 2026, read 10 November 2026. Box office is Box Office Mojo the same day, with The Numbers beside it. Disney's segments are the fiscal year ended 27 September 2025. [S017; S005; S019; S093]</p>
</div>

<p class="art-p">Five findings, each with a date on it:</p>
<ol>
<li>Merchandise, licensing and retail are 55.6% of the list (range 43.2–65.8). [S017]</li>
<li>Pokémon's row, $130.7 billion, is larger than Star Wars and the Marvel Cinematic Universe added together, $84.2 billion. [S017]</li>
<li>Five first-listed parents account for 58.3% of the list (range 52.3–72.7). Disney alone is 29.5%. [S017]</li>
<li>US-origin rows now sum to more than Japan-origin rows ($825.5 billion vs $394.5 billion). Japan's merchandise share is the higher one, 71.4% to 51.5%. [S017]</li>
<li>Nine of the ten biggest 2025 films worldwide were franchise installments. In 2026, so far, six of ten are. The denominator is ten films, and 2026 is not finished. [S005]</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="pokemon" class="anchored">How does Pokémon end up this large?</h2>
<p class="art-p">Pokémon is listed at $130.7 billion. Star Wars is $46.7 billion and the Marvel Cinematic Universe is $37.5 billion. Add those two film-world rows and you get $84.2 billion. You can bring a space station and a team of superheroes. On this list, the merchandise still needs counting. [S017]</p>
<figure class="art-chart">
<div class="art-chart-live art-chart-live--roomy" data-chart="/data/articles/franchise/charts/candidates/c02_pokemon_vs_two.plotly.json" data-fallback="/data/articles/franchise/charts/candidates/c02_pokemon_vs_two.png" data-source="Wikipedia rev 1384334611, read 10 Nov 2026 (S017)" role="img" aria-label="Pokémon at 130.7 billion beside Star Wars plus the MCU at 84.2 billion."></div>
<figcaption class="art-chart-caption art-chart-caption--visible">Adding two rows is not the same thing as merging two companies. It is still a startling piece of arithmetic.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p class="art-p">Across 140 properties the stated lifetime total is $1,460.1 billion. That is $12.0 billion more than the 12 October revision, and the whole of the increase is two rows, which we will come to. Mickey Mouse &amp; Friends follows Pokémon at $61.2 billion, then Winnie-the-Pooh at $50.2 billion, Star Wars, and Disney Princess at $45.4 billion. The small bear did not wait for a cinematic universe to get organised. [S017]</p>
<p class="art-p">Pokémon began in 1996 with Game Freak and Nintendo. Games, animation, cards and other products give people more than one way to stay with the same characters. That is a fair description of the system. It is not proof that every viewer becomes a card buyer. Jenkins's <em>Convergence Culture</em> describes stories moving across formats. Steinberg's <em>Anime's Media Mix</em> describes how Japanese animation and character goods grew up together. Japan's production committees can put publishers, broadcasters and toy makers into the same rights arrangement. [Jenkins, 2006; Steinberg, 2012; Hernández Hernández, 2018]</p>
<p class="art-p">Disney takes another route, through parks, resorts, cruises and shops. Different arrangements, more than one place to spend money. Neither story requires us to assume that anyone is being fooled. People can love the story and also want the toy.</p>

<h2 id="shopping" class="anchored">The film ends. The shopping does not.</h2>
<p class="art-p">The list puts merchandise, licensing and retail at $812.4 billion, video games at $268.2 billion and box office at $265.3 billion. Another $27.2 billion, about 1.9%, is in the totals without an itemised breakdown. [S017]</p>

<h2 id="hello-kitty" class="anchored">Did Hello Kitty really lose billions?</h2>
<p class="art-p">Not in the sense that sentence suggests. The 2019 file listed Hello Kitty at $80.0 billion. The November list says $33.5 billion. Mario went from $35.0 billion to $9.74 billion, and Gundam from $25.5 billion to $5.0 billion. Those rows did not move between 12 October and 7 November. The change is the long edit between the 2019 file and the current list, which this article has treated as revised sourcing rather than a reported collapse at Sanrio or Nintendo. [S001; S017]</p>
<figure class="art-chart">
<div class="art-chart-live art-chart-live--roomy" data-chart="/data/articles/franchise/charts/candidates/c03_revisions.plotly.json" data-fallback="/data/articles/franchise/charts/candidates/c03_revisions.png" data-source="S001 paired to S017" role="img" aria-label="2026 stated totals for Hello Kitty, Mario and Gundam after the source revision."></div>
<figcaption class="art-chart-caption art-chart-caption--visible">Before announcing a mascot recession, check whether the spreadsheet changed.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p class="art-p">The 2019 baseline is TidyTuesday's 2 July file: 103 franchises, audited against Wikipedia revision 904026364, which really is 29 June 2019. A hand-mapped crosswalk has 103 rows, and 82 of them have a usable total in both vintages. These are not two identical baskets watched for seven years. [S001; S007]</p>
<p class="art-p">Among paired rows, Pokémon rises from $90.9 billion to $130.7 billion, a difference of $39.8 billion. Naruto rises by about $13.9 billion. Call of Duty rises from $17.0 billion to $40.0 billion, a difference of $23.0 billion, and most of the new part of that story is below. Call these revenue growth only where the crosswalk says the scope matches. [S001; S017]</p>
<p class="art-p">The audit drew 40 franchises from the old file with seed 20261012. Thirty-nine matched the June 2019 page. Of those, 28 were within 10% of the Wikipedia headline, and 5 were within 1% of the itemised breakdown. The old spreadsheet was not an immaculate starting point either. We re-ran that check. The 28 of 39 is still the result. [S001; S007]</p>
<p class="art-p">Box office's share of the list moves from 8.9% in the 2019 file to 18.1% now, and a lot of that is fuller itemisation plus a different basket, not a sudden change in what people buy. [S001; S017] The companion piece keeps the revision log: <a href="/franchise-data-fix">What changed when we fixed the data</a>.</p>

<h2 id="what-moved" class="anchored">What moved since October?</h2>
<p class="art-p">We re-parsed the list. Same 140 names. Two stated totals changed. Call of Duty went from $31 billion to $40 billion. Paw Patrol went from $15.4 billion to $18.4 billion. Everything else in the comparison, including Pokémon, held still. [S017]</p>

<div class="art-chart-live art-chart-live--roomy" data-chart="/data/articles/franchise/charts/candidates/c04_what_moved.plotly.json" data-fallback="/data/articles/franchise/charts/candidates/c04_what_moved.png" data-source="S017, S094, S095" role="img" aria-label="Call of Duty up 9 billion and Paw Patrol up 3 billion since the October revision."></div>
<figcaption class="art-chart-caption art-chart-caption--visible">The whole list grew by $12.0 billion. These two rows are that $12.0 billion.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p class="art-p">Call of Duty's new row is a single video-game line of $40 billion. IGN reported on 26 October 2026 that the franchise had surpassed $40 billion in lifetime revenue, quoting a public note from Xbox. "Surpassed" is a threshold, and the list rounds it to $40 billion. It is not an itemised audit. [S094; S017]</p>
<p class="art-p">Paw Patrol's retail line is on firmer ground than the row's total. Deadline, 28 October 2026, says the franchise has $18 billion in lifetime retail, in a story about a fourth film. The Wikipedia row then adds a box-office line of $373.7 million and a home-media line of $39 million, both cited to The Numbers pages with access dates in 2024 and 2023, and prints $18.4 billion. The same Deadline story says one of the films grossed more than half a billion dollars worldwide, which does not sit neatly under that older box-office line. We keep the list's stated $18.4 billion in the sum, and we do not pretend the extra $0.4 billion was freshly checked. [S095; S017]</p>

<h2 id="region-origin" class="anchored">Is Japan the franchise giant, or is the United States?</h2>
<p class="art-p">On this list, US-origin franchises have the larger total. Japan-origin franchises have the higher merchandise share. That is narrower than declaring a winner between two national industries, which this dataset cannot do. [S017]</p>

<p class="art-p">The 2019 file had the totals the other way around: Japan $681.5 billion, the United States $622.6 billion, and the merchandise shares the other way too, 49.1% for Japan and 64.6% for the United States. Countries did not swap business models while a CSV was downloading. The basket changed. [S001]</p>
<p class="art-p">An earlier draft of this article said 43 additional US-origin properties. The crosswalk does not support 43. It marks 45 US-origin rows on the November list that have no 2019 row, including Fortnite, Avatar, Madden NFL, Scooby-Doo and The Sims, and also separate rows for Thor, Iron Man, Captain America and Black Panther. Japanese additions are fewer, five rows, and several large Japanese estimates were revised down. English-language Wikipedia is not a census of world culture. [S001; S017]</p>
<p class="art-p">Region here means the creator's home market at launch, not where the money was spent. The rebuilt MCU row can be mostly theatrical while Pokémon, Anpanman and Hello Kitty pull Japan's merchandise share up. Some of those character rows sit beside the MCU row, so adding every hero's row to the MCU total would count the same affection twice. [S017]</p>

<h2 id="disney" class="anchored">How much of the table sits with Disney?</h2>
<p class="art-p">First decide what "Disney" includes. On the <strong>2019 file</strong>, the owner string "The Walt Disney Company" sums to $259.0 billion. Roll in the subsidiaries the way our rule does and the same file sums to $426.2 billion. The standalone Nintendo label, which does not include the Pokémon Company string, is $49.0 billion. Leaving the rooms uncounted is a good way to lose the house. Combining them still needs a rule, especially when rights are shared. [S001]</p>

<p class="art-p">A purchase price is not lifetime consumer spending. Putting them near each other does not produce a return, and it does not tell us whether a deal was cheap. It starts a question about what the buyer thought it was buying.</p>

<p class="art-p">The domestic coding, which counts titles rather than ticket dollars, runs from 60% franchise entries in the 2010 top ten to 80% in 2019 and 90% in 2024. We did not recode 2025's domestic chart in this pass, so the line stops at 2024 on purpose. [S005]</p>

<h2 id="failure-modes" class="anchored">Can a familiar name still miss?</h2>
<p class="art-p">Owning a recognisable character does not make the next release work. The case list points at Universal shelving the Dark Universe after <em>The Mummy</em> (2017), the Fantastic Beasts films falling short of Harry Potter's peaks, DC's changes before the 2023 DC Studios reset, the reception of <em>Ghostbusters</em> (2016), and the changing fortunes of Angry Birds. Links are in <code>franchise_failures.csv</code>. [S013; S015]</p>
<p class="art-p">Those cases are invitations to look, not a single cause called franchise fatigue, and not a moral about discipline. A lifetime total does not say whether the latest release made money. The spreadsheet remembers past success. Audiences are under no obligation to renew it.</p>

<h2 id="own-or-market-an-ip" class="anchored">What this means if you own or market an IP</h2>
<p class="art-p">This section is for brand, licensing and marketing teams. It turns the figures above into questions to ask and cautions to keep. It uses only numbers already in this report, and it is not advice on any single deal. [S017]</p>

<h3 id="ip-consolidation" class="anchored">Ownership is concentrated, so check who sits behind a property</h3>
<p class="art-p">On the November list, Disney is the first-listed parent for $430.7 billion, 29.5% of the list (range 18.0–42.9). The top five parents are 58.3% (range 52.3–72.7). [S017]</p>
<p class="art-p">Part of that concentration was bought. The filed prices we checked are $7.4 billion for Pixar in 2006, about $4.0 billion for Marvel in 2009, $4.05 billion for Lucasfilm in 2012, and $71.3 billion in cash and stock for 21st Century Fox in a 2018 exhibit. [S009–S012] On the 2019 file, the owner name "The Walt Disney Company" alone summed to $259.0 billion. With the subsidiaries rolled in, the same file sums to $426.2 billion. [S001] A name on a character can understate who controls it.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Check the rights holder for the specific property, not the studio name.</strong> Rights are sometimes shared. Spider-Man's film and merchandise rights connect Disney/Marvel and Sony in different ways, and Pokémon's copyright is shared by Nintendo, Game Freak and Creatures. [S017]</li>
<li><strong>Ask how the holder's other properties overlap your category.</strong> Five parents sit behind 58.3% of the list, so a licensor with a large portfolio is a normal case, not an exception. [S017]</li>
<li><strong>Treat an acquisition as a change in who you deal with.</strong> It is not evidence that the property will sell the way it did before. A purchase price is not lifetime consumer spending, and buying a company does not retroactively earn the money audiences once spent on its characters. [S009–S012]</li>
<li><strong>Quote the counting rule with the share.</strong> On the 2019 file with today's parent labels, the top five were 62.8% in one of our checks and 46.4% before the roll-up. Consolidation changes the answer. [S001]</li>
</ul>

<h3 id="ip-merchandise" class="anchored">Merchandise is the main engine, but it works differently in different places</h3>
<p class="art-p">Merchandise, licensing and retail are $812.4 billion, 55.6% of the list (range 43.2–65.8). Video games are 18.3% and box office 18.1%, and those two ranges overlap. [S017] Pokémon's $130.7 billion row is larger than Star Wars and the Marvel Cinematic Universe added together, $84.2 billion. [S017]</p>
<p class="art-p">By origin, the 30 Japan-origin franchises total $394.5 billion with a merchandise share of 71.4%. The 88 US-origin franchises total $825.5 billion with 51.5%. The nine UK-origin franchises are $136.1 billion at 53.9%. [S017] We read the gap as two merchandising styles, not two national industries. Pokémon, Anpanman and Hello Kitty pull Japan's share up, while the MCU row can be mostly theatrical. [S017] Steinberg describes Japanese animation and character goods growing up together, and Japan's production committees can put publishers, broadcasters and toy makers into one rights arrangement. [Steinberg, 2012; Hernández Hernández, 2018] Disney's own filing shows a different route: for the year ended 27 September 2025, Experiences, which includes parks, resorts, cruises and merchandise, was $36.2 billion beside Entertainment at $42.5 billion. [S093]</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Decide which style your property is closer to before you benchmark.</strong> A character-led property, where the goods are much of the product, should not be measured against a story-led property using one 55.6% figure. [S017]</li>
<li><strong>Do not credit merchandise to a single campaign or film.</strong> The list counts toys, games and tickets separately, but they are not independent of each other. A toy can be the thing the film was for. [S017]</li>
<li><strong>Do not turn 71.4% and 51.5% into national traits.</strong> Region means the creator's home market at launch. The basket also changed: the crosswalk marks 45 US-origin rows on the November list that have no 2019 row. [S017; S001]</li>
</ul>

<h3 id="ip-caveats" class="anchored">What the data cannot tell you</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>It is not profit.</strong> The figures are nominal estimates of consumer spending, compiled by Wikipedia's editors. They are not profit, not a company valuation, and not cash paid to a rights holder. [S017]</li>
<li><strong>The ranges are wide.</strong> Disney's share runs 18.0–42.9 and the top five's 52.3–72.7. The ranges show how much a share depends on which rows are on the list. They are not sampling error, and they do not certify the underlying estimates. [S017]</li>
<li><strong>The estimates move.</strong> Between 12 October and 7 November, Call of Duty went from $31 billion to $40 billion and Paw Patrol from $15.4 billion to $18.4 billion, which is all of the list's $12.0 billion rise. [S017] Hello Kitty's row went from $80.0 billion in the 2019 file to $33.5 billion, a revised row, not a reported collapse at Sanrio. [S001; S017]</li>
<li><strong>Small groups swing.</strong> Canada's two franchises sum to $23.1 billion with a 77.9% merchandise share, and Sweden's three sum to $26.3 billion with 12.8%. One row changes either figure. [S017]</li>
<li><strong>A lifetime total says nothing about the next release.</strong> The list has no royalty rates, licence terms, margins or campaign results, and a famous name can still miss. [S013; S015]</li>
</ul>
<p class="art-p">Before you quote a number from this report, give its vintage (the Wikipedia revision and the date we read it), give the range, say that it is an estimate of consumer spending, and check the <a href="/franchise/changelog">changelog</a> for a later revision. [S017]</p>

<h2 id="takeaway" class="anchored">What to keep</h2>
<p class="art-p">Counting only movies leaves out most of this list. Merchandise, licensing and retail are 55.6% of the recorded lifetime spending, inside a range wide enough to respect. Pokémon's $130.7 billion is larger than Star Wars and the MCU combined. Five assigned parents sit behind 58.3% of the table, on a rule that is not a title search. [S017]</p>
<p class="art-p">Every useful comparison needs a date, a denominator and a description of what the number measures. Consumer spending is not profit. A changed estimate is not a collapsing business. A national label is not a national industry. Buying a company does not retroactively earn the money audiences once spent on its characters.</p>
<p class="art-p">The characters may travel between games, films, shelves and parks. The accounting labels should travel with them.</p>
