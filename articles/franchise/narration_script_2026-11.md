Before the big numbers get comfortable, a warning. These are estimates of what audiences spent, gathered by Wikipedia's editors over many years. They are not profit, and they are not a cheque to the rights holder. A large number can still be the answer to a different question.

The list we read on the tenth of November, 2026, is Wikipedia's revision from the seventh. One hundred and forty franchises. One thousand, four hundred and sixty billion dollars, in nominal lifetime estimates.

Merchandise, licensing and retail are fifty-five point six percent. The range around that share runs from about forty-three percent to about sixty-six. Video games are eighteen point three percent. Box office is eighteen point one. Those two ranges overlap, so we will not crown a runner-up.

Pokémon is one hundred and thirty point seven billion. Star Wars and the Marvel Cinematic Universe, added together, are eighty-four point two. You may bring the space station and the superheroes. On this list, the merchandise still needs counting.

Hello Kitty did not lose a fortune in the way that sentence sounds. The old file said eighty billion. The current list says thirty-three point five. Mario and Gundam moved the same way. The spreadsheet changed its sourcing. The companies did not publish a matching collapse.

Since October, only two rows moved. Call of Duty, to a rounded forty billion, after Xbox said the franchise had surpassed that mark. Paw Patrol, to the list's eighteen point four billion. Deadline confirms eighteen billion of retail. The remaining slice uses older box-office citations, and we say so.

The United States has the larger sum. Japan has the higher merchandise share. That is not a contest between two countries' entire cultures.

Nine of the ten biggest films of 2025 were already part of a franchise. The trackers do not agree what Ne Zha 2 grossed, and we refuse to average them. In 2026, so far, six of the ten biggest are franchise films. The year is not over. Ten films are not the whole market.

Disney's latest full year, ended in September 2025, shows Experiences as a real business: thirty-six billion dollars of segment revenue, beside Entertainment and Sports. That is a year. The Wikipedia rows are a lifetime of estimates. They are not the same object.

Keep the date, the denominator, and the definition. Then the joke, if there is one, is already in the facts.
